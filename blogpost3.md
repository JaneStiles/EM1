<p>Man With A Movie Camera

My main take aways from Man With A Movie Camera directed by Dziga Vertov is that he shows the subjectivity and process of filmmaking, revealing the maker behind the movie. There are many shots that show the mechanisms that make a camera, these images are cut against industrial work places underscoring that making a film is work and constructed like any other job. Through double exposure, he shows the cinematographer co-existing amongst people in the city, documenting everyday life without romanticizing. He films different areas of society, i.e. people signing marriage and divorce papers, a birth, and a woman getting her hair and nails done. 

The film ends with the camera pointing at the audience, which points to how the viewer also constructs a narrative. At the time this film was made, film as a medium was newer in society. Vertov highlights that the camera is not objective, film is crafted by a maker and can direct where the audience's eye looks and experiences reality. It’s a didactic film that makes one think about how the filmmaker has a tremendous amount of power in shaping cultural memory, which is still true today. 
</p>
