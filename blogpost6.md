The effects of technology are like a double edged sword. “Hostility from the oppressed is rechanneled into the bureaucracy, Which misdirects antagonism away from the nomadic power field. The retreat into the invisibility of nonlocation prevents those caught in the panoptic spatial lock-down from defining a site of resistance (a theater of operations).” (Pg.16) This quote makes me think of social media activism. While it’s great that networks and people can connect and share information, there is often a lot of screaming into the void, posturing and toxic arguing that keeps people from actually resisting the powers that be. I have definitely fallen into this trap. Social media can create a false sense of doing something. When people meet in person to organize and take action so much more nuance can be held and so much more can get accomplished. 


“The location of power—and the site of resistance—rest in an ambiguous zone without borders.” Pg. 11 the positive aspect of the digital world is how people can connect internationally. On a personal level social media has helped me in my political education especially since Covid. I follow activists, artists and organizers that share resources that I can study and actions I can tap into. I think about marginalized people in the US who live in conservative towns, they can find hope and sometimes mutual aid via the internet.
