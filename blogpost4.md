The theatre that grew out of Futurism inspired a mob mentality in the audience and led to political commitment. “People struggle at the doors to get in, they discuss and argue in front of the pictures; they are either before or against, they take sides, they say what they think at the tops of their voices….it is a tumult of cries, shouts, bursts of laughter, protests.” pg. 46 Futurism ushered in an active embrace of right wing nationalism. It makes me think of Trump rallies and his Christian base. The theatrics of Evangelical church services provokes crowds of people to join in on the experience. The theatrics of his campaign won votes and support from millions of people in the United States. “Futurism’s innovation was not so much about empowering the audience as harnessing and redirecting its energy and attention…symbiosis between an artistic embrace of violence audiences who wanted to be part of a work of art and feel legitimated to participate in its violence.” pg. 46 These plays, like propaganda, rewrote historical memory and engaged audiences to participate, which speaks to the need that people have to want to be part of a movement.
